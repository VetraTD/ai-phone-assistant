01

The 30-second version

Two programs share one database. One answers phones; one is the website where businesses set up their receptionist.

A customer calls a business's number. Twilio (the phone company) answers and opens a live audio pipe to your voice server. The server streams the caller's speech to Deepgram (which turns speech into text), sends that text to Gemini (Google's AI, which decides what to say and whether to do anything — book, take a message, transfer), and streams the reply to ElevenLabs (which turns it back into a human voice) and out to the caller. That loop repeats every time the caller speaks. Everything — who called, the transcript, any appointment or message — is written to Supabase (the database). The business owner sees all of it, and configures their receptionist, in the dashboard.

Why the greeting is instant: the server only waits to identify the business before speaking. Loading the FAQ, past-caller history, and creating the call record all happen in the background while it's already saying hello — so the caller never hears dead air at pickup.

03

The listen–think–speak loop

This is the engine. One trip through it = one back-and-forth. It's built to feel like talking to a person, which mostly comes down to speed and knowing when it's your turn.

HEAR

Speech → text

Caller's audio is transcribed live, word by word, as they talk.

Deepgram Nova-3
YIELD

Whose turn?

Decides when the caller has actually finished — and lets "wait/stop" interrupt but ignores "uh-huh."

turn manager + VAD
THINK

Decide reply

The AI reads the conversation + the business's info and writes a short answer, calling a tool if needed.

Gemini 2.5 Flash
SPEAK

Text → voice

The reply streams out as a human voice as it's being written — no waiting for the whole sentence.

ElevenLabs Flash

Three design choices make it feel human rather than robotic. It streams at every stage — the AI starts talking before it has finished thinking, so first sound comes back in a fraction of a second. It handles interruptions honestly — say "wait" and it stops; say "yeah, mm-hmm" and it keeps going, because those are just you nodding along. And it protects against its own echo — on speakerphone the AI can hear itself, so it cross-checks the audio energy with what you actually said before deciding you interrupted it.

Every turn is timed. The server records how long each stage took (heard → thought → spoke → played) so you can see, per call, whether it's hitting the sub-second target. That's the /api/debug/latency endpoint.

04

Transfers, fallbacks & safety nets

The loop above handles a normal conversation. These are the branches for when something needs a human, or when a piece breaks.

↗

Transfer to a human Live

The AI briefly says it's transferring, then Twilio dials the business's transfer number and bridges the caller to a real person. Works in any language (the AI decides to transfer), with an instant English keyword shortcut for "representative/manager/human."

caller asks for a person · or AI can't help

Billing note: a transferred call runs two phone legs at once (caller + the human) until someone hangs up.

✎

Take a message Live · always on

The universal safety net. Any time the AI can't help — unknown answer, after hours, caller just wants to leave word — it captures name, callback number (read back digit by digit), reason, and urgency, then promises a callback. The message lands in the database and notifies the business.

"can I leave a message" · or any dead end
◈

Brain-down fallback Live

If the AI itself fails twice in a call (Gemini errors or times out), the receptionist switches to a scripted, no-AI message-taker — fixed questions, no thinking required — so the caller still gets to leave their details instead of hearing "sorry, try again" on a loop.

2 consecutive AI failures
♪

Voice fallback Live

If ElevenLabs fails (or you run out of credits), speech automatically falls back to Google's voice for the rest of the call. The caller hears a more robotic voice, but the call never drops.

ElevenLabs error / no credits
⏱

Silence & max-length Live

If the caller goes quiet, it nudges ("are you still there?") at 8s and 15s, then gently ends the call at 24s. Any call is hard-capped at 30 minutes.

no speech · or 30-min cap
☂

Degraded / voicemail Built · off

A last-resort mode: if the whole voice stack is down, play an apology and record a plain voicemail into the message inbox. It's coded but nothing triggers it automatically yet — a known gap on the fix list.

voice stack unavailable (not wired)

05

What the receptionist can actually do

The AI is given a set of "tools" per call. Some are always on for every business; others are switches the business flips in settings; a third kind connects to outside systems.

Core always on

  • answer questions from the FAQ / business info
  • message take a message with callback details
  • callback log a callback request
  • transfer hand off to a human

Modules per-business toggles

  • book create an appointment
  • check look up the caller's appointments
  • cancel/resched change one (with identity check)
  • quote request a quote
  • directions · forms info requests

Integrations coded, no UI

  • webhook call a business's own API as a tool
  • Athena EHR: real appointment slots for medical offices
  • backend works; no dashboard screen to set them up yet

Identity & safety

  • cancel/reschedule needs name + last-4 of the number on file
  • bookings are validated: no past dates, no closed hours
  • won't invent prices, hours, or coverage it doesn't know
How a "tool" works: when the AI decides to, say, book an appointment, it doesn't just talk about it — it emits a structured tool call that the server runs against the database, gets back success or failure, and speaks the honest result. It's told never to claim success unless the tool actually returned it.

06

After the call hangs up

When Twilio reports the call ended, a second flow runs to wrap it up.

▤

Transcript & summary Live

The full transcript is already saved line-by-line during the call. On hang-up, the AI writes a one-line summary, a sentiment, and an outcome (booked / message / transferred / spam) onto the call record.

✉

Notify the business Live

If enabled, the owner gets an email and/or SMS when a message or booking comes in — to the notification address set in the dashboard.

↩

SMS follow-up to the caller Built · off

Text the caller after the call: an appointment confirmation, a "we got your message" receipt, or a missed-call text-back. Fully coded, but there's no dashboard toggle to turn it on yet, so it stays dormant until that switch is added (it's on the fix list).

needs the settings toggle wired
◔

Spam handling Live

A call that connects but no one ever speaks, and ends in under 8 seconds, is marked spam — it skips the AI summary (saves cost) and is flagged in the call list.

Getting a number: during onboarding, the dashboard asks the voice server to buy a Twilio number and wire its webhooks automatically, then saves it on the business row. That's the one time the two apps talk — through a small API on the voice server.

08

What's not built — so you're not surprised

Being straight about the edges of the system.

📵

Outbound calls Not built

The receptionist only answers calls. It never places one on its own. The only "outward" actions are transferring a caller who's already on the line, and sending SMS. There's no outbound campaign / reminder-call system.

📅

Real-time Google Calendar Manual only

Phone bookings write to the database. They only appear in the owner's Google Calendar when someone clicks "sync" in the dashboard — not automatically. A decision to make, not a bug.

🌐

Multilingual scripts Partial

The AI can understand and answer in other languages, but the fixed lines (greeting, nudges, goodbye, the whole brain-down script) are English-only. A Spanish caller hears English at those moments.

⚙

Integrations & SMS UI No screen

Webhook/EHR integrations and caller SMS follow-up work in code but have no dashboard screen to turn on — so they're effectively off until those settings are added.
